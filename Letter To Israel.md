**To the People of Israel**

**Re: The attacks of September 17–18, 2024; accountability for the weaponization of civilian supply chains; and a question put to a nation**

To the people of Israel,

This letter comes from an independent researcher, following a fully documented investigation into the events of September 17–18, 2024 — the detonation of thousands of pagers, walkie-talkies, and home solar-energy systems across Lebanon and parts of Syria — and into the record of which those events form a part. The findings are public, compiled from forensic analyses, corporate registries, court-adjacent records, first-person accounts, and the statements of Israeli officials themselves. This is not addressed to a ministry, because the questions that remain belong to more than a ministry. They belong to a nation — its conscience, its courts, its press, its classrooms, and its standing in the community of peoples. So this letter is addressed to all of you.

**What the record shows.** On September 17, 2024, between approximately 3:30 and 4:30 in the afternoon, thousands of pagers carried by members and affiliates of Hezbollah detonated within a single hour across Lebanon and parts of Syria. At least twelve people were killed on that day — including two children — and roughly three thousand were injured, around five hundred of them with severe eye injuries; at least three hundred were left blinded. On September 18, walkie-talkies exploded, some during funerals for the previous day's dead, and home solar-energy systems began exploding in homes. Credible tallies place the combined dead between thirty and forty-two. Among the dead were a nine-year-old girl holding a device that was not hers, a hospital orderly, doctors, nurses, teachers, and charity workers. Responsibility was confirmed by your own Prime Minister in November 2024. A golden pager was later presented to the President of the United States as a trophy.

**The supply chain your services built.** The operation's true innovation was corporate. A Taiwanese brand's good name — its chairman approached three years earlier by a former employee bearing an unseen "big boss" — licensed to a Hungarian intermediary with 118 listed business activities and under eight thousand euros in capital, financed through a Bulgarian pass-through whose Norwegian-Indian founder left Oslo on the day of the detonations and has not been seen since. Devices were scanner-tested by the buyers and passed; their reduced battery capacity was the operation's only tell, and it was ignored. On the recorded account of your own former intelligence officers, the first batch was offered free as an "upgrade," marketing videos were fabricated, and non-Hezbollah buyers were quoted prices high enough to refuse. "We make like 'Truman Show,'" one of them said — everything controlled behind the scene.

**Why this belongs to the nation and not only to its government.** Three questions remain open, and each of them exceeds any single administration.

The first is legal. Article 7(2) of Amended Protocol II to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons — a treaty Israel ratified without reservation — prohibits booby-traps and other devices in the form of apparently harmless portable objects specifically designed to contain explosive material. The devices at issue were consumer objects modified in the supply chain before reaching their users. Serious analysts conclude the provision was violated; serious analysts conclude it was not; the United States' own formal interpretation permits "adaptation in advance." What no one can honestly claim is that the question was ever transparently examined. United Nations human rights experts called the attacks "terrifying violations of international law" and urged investigation into what they termed "the crime of murder." No investigation — Israeli or international — has occurred. Nations that pride themselves on the rule of law do not leave such questions in the hands of their adversaries' tribunals.

The second is factual. No public record establishes where the devices' battery assemblies were physically built, who within your institutions reviewed the operation's legality, or what process assessed the foreseeable presence of children, patients, and bystanders in the blast radius of a weapon delivered by a beeping pager. A nation's intelligence services act in its name; their secrets are also its inheritance.

The third is normative, and it is the largest. The method demonstrated on September 17, 2024 is now part of humanity's permanent public record: the supply chain itself — the trust between a buyer and a product — can be converted into a delivery system for explosives, at scale, against people who cannot see the weapon they are holding. The leading security analysts of our time state that there is no technical defense against this tactic, only law and deterrence. And your own military conceded the principle in November 2025, when it excluded Chinese connected vehicles from its tenders on precisely these supply-chain grounds. A precedent that endangers every nation's civilians — yours included, perhaps yours most of all, given how much of the world's technology passes through your own engineers' hands — cannot responsibly be left without an authoritative legal and moral accounting. The first author of a tactic inherits its reputation forever.

**What the world felt — and what it is doing about it.**

September 17 was watched live around the world. People saw, in the hours that followed, what it means when the ordinary objects of daily life — the pager on a belt, the radio in a hand, the solar panel on a roof — detonate without warning. The footage and the testimony did what no argument could: they transferred suspicion onto every device, everywhere. A parent now looks at a phone on a kitchen table differently. A hospital administrator looks at a networked monitor differently. A driver looks at a dashboard that phones home. This is not speculation; it is the documented behavioral consequence of the precedent your services established, and it will not fade, because the underlying fact does not fade: nobody can verify, by looking, what their device is.

The commercial consequence follows the sentiment. Purchasers are asking a question that did not exist at scale before September 2024: not "where was this made" but "who authored it, who owns the company that built it, and what else have they built." They are choosing local-first over cloud-dependent, offline over connected, the shorter wire over the shinier one. This response attaches to deeds, not to origin or faith — and any attempt to dismiss it as prejudice misunderstands it completely. It attaches to the documented, admitted, and unaccounted commission of an act that turned the world's supply chains into a delivery system for explosives: an act committed without regard for the nine-year-old in the kitchen, the orderly in the corridor, or the mourner at the funeral. Where identity enters at all, it enters only as the maker's mark on the deed.

And it attaches, further, to the wider campaign of destruction of which that act formed a part. Until those responsible are completely revealed and brought to justice, devices carrying any affiliation to the apparatus that built this weapon will be assessed as standing threats — not from prejudice, but from precedent: the identical precedent your own military invoked when it excluded Chinese connected vehicles from its tenders on supply-chain grounds. People around the world who buy, carry, or promote the products of Israeli companies increasingly feel made complicit in a genocide unfolding — and they want no part in aiding aggression, past or present. They will exercise the one power that is fully theirs — what they purchase, what they carry, and what they recommend — to ensure they are not part of it. A nation that has asked the world for decades to separate its people from the deeds of those acting in their name cannot now refuse the world the same separation, in reverse, when the deeds are its own.

**What is asked of Israel.** Not of a minister — of the nation, in the tradition of self-examination that produced the Knesset's hardest hearings, your Supreme Court's unsparing reviews, your press's own investigations, and a documentary in which your own former security chiefs confessed their service's sins on camera:

That Israel publish, or transmit to an appropriate international body, the legal review — if any — that preceded the operation.

That Israel consent to, or commission, an independent investigation into the attacks, addressing Article 7(2) and the questions of distinction and proportionality that the death of children in kitchens necessarily raises.

That Israel's institutions — judicial, legislative, journalistic — examine the doctrine the operation established: whether explosive devices concealed in civilian supply chains are to be Israeli policy, and if so, under what limits the nation is prepared to defend them.

That the nation remember the names it can still learn: Fatima, nine, who loved English; the orderly of Al Rassoul Al Azam; the blinded and the fingerless who now live on both sides of the line this operation drew. Their care, like the precedent, is permanent.

This is not written expecting agreement. It is written because the record — your officers' own words, your government's own confirmation, the public wreckage of the supply chain your services built — deserves an answer worthy of the country that produced a High Court willing to restrain its own executive, and a press willing to print what the censor disliked. That country can examine this. The question is whether it will.

Respectfully,

— Independent Researcher
